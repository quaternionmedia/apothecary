# Proposed rule — Light Language

**Status:** proposed, nobody has agreed to it yet
**Applies to:** every project, not just this one
**Where it has to move to:** the shared rulebook, at the top level

## The rule

Every set of documents has a plain page at the front. It is written first, and
everything else is reached through it.

Plain means: a person who does not do this work can read it start to finish and
come away knowing what the thing is, what it does, and what is unresolved. Not a
summary of the real documents. The way in.

## Why this is a rule and not a preference

Right now the way in to any of this work is a document written for someone who
already knows the work. That is a door that only opens from the inside. It shuts
out anyone deciding whether to fund it, join it, use it, or trust it — which is
most of the people whose opinion matters.

Writing the plain page afterwards does not fix this. A summary written last
inherits the shape of what it summarises, and the shape is the problem. Written
first, the plain page forces the question *what is this actually for*, and that
question improves the technical documents too.

## What plain means, in practice

**Write for someone outside.** Not a beginner in the field — a person who does
not work in the field at all and has no plans to.

**Explain a word where you use it, or don't use it.** If a term is not one a
reader would say out loud in conversation, either replace it or say what it means
in the same sentence. Never send them to a list of definitions at the end. If
they have to look something up, the sentence has already failed.

**Names are fine; unexplained names are not.** Products, tools and projects have
names, and pretending otherwise is worse. The first time one appears, say what it
does in ordinary words. After that, use the name freely.

**Spell out short forms or drop them.** Prefer dropping them. A set of initials
that saves four words costs a reader far more than four words.

**Say the thing, not the framing.** The hardest failures use no special words at
all. "The reviewer is the bottleneck" is perfectly ordinary English and still
only makes sense from inside. It is a way of talking, not a vocabulary.

**Deep pages stay deep.** This rule is about the entrance. A document written for
people building the thing should be written for people building the thing. It
just must not be the first thing anyone meets.

## How it gets checked

**The read-aloud test, and it is the real one.** Read the page to someone who
does not do this work. Every place they stop you, ask a question, or go quiet is
a fault in the page. Fix those places. This needs a person, and there is no
version of it that does not.

**A watch list of words, advisory only.** A short, growing list of terms that
usually signal trouble. A tool may flag them. **It may never pass or fail a
document.** The list is there to catch the easy half. It cannot catch the half
that matters, because that half is phrasing and assumption rather than
vocabulary, and a document can score perfectly against any list and still be
unreadable to the person it was written for. Anyone treating a clean flag report
as a passing grade has misunderstood the rule.

Under the existing rule that a claim to be checked must name its checker: the
checker here is a person doing the read-aloud test. The word list is a helper and
is described as one. That is weaker than the checks used elsewhere in this
rulebook, and it is the honest description.

## What follows if this is agreed

- Every project needs a plain front page. Most do not have one.
- Where a project's front page is generated automatically, the plain page is a
  separate page the generator includes, not something typed over the top of
  generated text.
- New work writes the plain page first. Existing work adds one when it is next
  touched, rather than in a single sweep nobody has time for.
- Documents already written stay as they are underneath. Nothing is rewritten
  for its own sake.

## What would make us drop or change this

- The read-aloud test gets skipped twice running because there was nobody to read
  to. Then the rule is decorative and needs either a real check or an honest
  demotion.
- Someone finds the plain page and the deep page saying different things. That is
  the failure this rule risks: two descriptions that drift apart. If it happens,
  the answer is probably one document with layers rather than two documents.
- The word list starts being used as a gate by anyone, including a tool. That
  means the rule has been misread and the list should be deleted rather than
  argued about.
