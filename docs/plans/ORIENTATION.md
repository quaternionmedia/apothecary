# Orientation

One page. What the organisation's rules are, and where this work touches them.

## The rules

Quaternion Media keeps a written rulebook: a set of documents saying what it has
decided and why. Every project follows it. A project may be stricter than it,
never looser. One person maintains all of it, which is the main thing to know.

Each decision is written the same way — what the constraints were, what was
decided, what it costs, what else was considered and the real reason each of
those lost, and the events that would force a rethink. A decision only becomes
final when a person signs it off, and **nothing has been signed off yet**.
Signing off is meant to need a second person, and there isn't one.

## What the rules make you do

| Rule | What it means day to day |
|---|---|
| Write the decision down or it didn't happen | No meaningful choice ships without a page explaining it |
| Own what you depend on | If an outside provider vanished tomorrow, the fix must be changing a setting, not rebuilding |
| Don't write the specialist parts | Pick those. Spend your effort on the connections between them, and keep those swappable |
| An unfinished page has no history | While it is still a draft, rewrite it as though you always thought this. The saved change history is the record of how the thinking moved |
| Credit names people | Software assistance is noted as a tool, never as an author. No name or address that no human reads |
| If you say something is checked, name the checker | And the checker has to already exist |
| Show it by running it | The demonstration falls out of something that had to be correct anyway |
| One open proposal per person per project | The person reviewing is the limit, not the person writing |

Three more are proposed and not yet agreed: every set of documents gets a
[plain page at the front](proposals/light-language.md), the software
[runs on your machine and keeps your data there](proposals/runs-and-stays-local.md),
and where a person and a machine can both judge something,
[each does the part they are good at](proposals/people-and-machines.md) — the
machine counts and works out what to ask, you answer, and your word wins.

## This work

**Photos into parts.** Photograph something; the tool finds the basic shapes,
places them in space, names each one so it can be reused, and assembles them into
arrangements that connect to each other. Several photographs can go in at once,
and the tool works out which are of the same thing — refusing to guess when it
cannot tell, which it says out loud rather than quietly joining them. It is much
worse at that than you are, so it asks: it works out which few questions are
worth your minute, and takes your answer as final.

**The ring is not a menu.** Everything measurable about it only makes sense if
its job is to replace an application's scattered controls with one way in. Now
written down, with a number a machine takes: twenty controls of its own today,
none after. An earlier draft of this page said twelve. Twelve was a hand count
and it was wrong.

## Where it touches the rules

| # | What it touches | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The first work to bring personal photographs anywhere near the organisation, and no rule covers that.** The existing rules ask whether an outside provider could be swapped. They say nothing about what information reaches one. A photo of a garage holds people, addresses, number plates, and a list of what you own | Now answered sideways: the proposed local-only rule means nothing leaves at all, so no separate photo rule is needed. The wider gap stays open for other projects |
| 2 | A belief nobody had written down becomes a rule with a measurable test | Proposed, in the ring project |
| 3 | Recognising shapes in a photo is exactly the kind of specialist part you pick rather than write | Connection built, choice deliberately not made. Local-only narrows the field on its own |
| 4 | Three overdue items this project owes: a list of outside services it uses, a list of what could go wrong, and a threshold for when a connection has quietly grown into a specialist part | All three closable by rewriting one existing unfinished page |
| 5 | Two places the rules contradict themselves: no category exists for the most ordinary act in the process; an automated check wanted credit given to an address no human reads | Both written down. The second was refused |
| 6 | The new rule in row 2 needs a person to check it, not a machine | Said plainly in the rule itself, as the rules require |

## What is not true yet

- **Nothing is decided.** Everything here is a proposal.
- **The first part is built**; the rest is described rather than written. What
  runs: pointing at a picture, finding flat shapes, naming them, and placing
  them. What does not: any change to the way you click things.
- **Four related projects are unknown, not absent.** They could not be reached
  from where this was written. From outside, a private project and one that was
  never created look identical. One request from someone with access settles it.
