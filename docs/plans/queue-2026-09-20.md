## 4. Hot versus cold: a bed probed at two temperatures, as time series

**The question.** A bed reading taken cold and one taken at printing
temperature differ: an aluminium bed grows and bows as it warms, a few
tenths of a millimetre on a 220 mm bed, which is the whole tolerance of a
first layer. The seam already keeps what is needed to see it -- every bed
reading is a record with `at`, `bed_c`, `hotend_c`, `method`, and
`stats` (range, tilt X and Y, each corner against the mean) -- and it is
the simplest real example of **viewing time-series data in the world**:
the same tethered popup, with a time axis.

**The test, on the bench** (the checklist's D, done twice):

1. Cold: *Read mesh* (the stored mesh, 14 °C bed) and *Probe bed* --
   two records at room temperature.
2. Bed to 60 °C from the control pad, soak five minutes (the chart shows
   when it is flat), *Probe bed* again -- a record at temperature.
3. Compare: the two probe records' range and corners. Expect the hot
   reading's range to differ by 0.1–0.3 mm and the tilt to change sign
   or size at the corners nearest the bed's clips.

**What to build** (queued, small, not started):

- In the bed-level section of the machine: a **Readings** chart under
  the history -- x is time, one series is range (mm), a second is bed
  temperature (°C, the chart's blue), each reading a dot; a click on a dot
  shows that reading (the heatmap, and the relief in the world). The temp
  chart's SVG code is the pattern (`renderChart` in
  `widgets/machine.js`), sixty lines.
- On the simulator: a bed reading taken with the bed at 60 °C records
  `bed_c` 60, so a browser test can take a cold and a hot reading and see
  two dots at two temperatures.
- The same chart then generalises: temperatures over a print, a print's
  progress, probe drift over days -- one time-series widget, mounted where
  its data has a place in the world.
- Not built: the firmware does not report a mesh's temperature, so the
  record's `bed_c` is the temperature *at the reading*, which is what the
  test wants; a mesh stored days ago and read cold is a cold reading.

**Next step:** the two probes on the bench (they are the checklist's D,
twice); the chart follows the data.
