# Changelog

One line per change, each with a link to where it is described. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). [Unreleased] is the net
change since 0.1.0: what is there now that was not, and what 0.1.0 had that is gone.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `apothecary serve --demo` goes round every loop with nothing real: printer_1's mainboard pinned to an idle simulated Ender, a simulated ESP32 devkit, the scripted arduino-cli, cargo and espflash, and the OrcaSlicer installed (or Slice says how to install one), on a state folder of its own, `.cache/demo` in the checkout, kept between runs. A line above the header says it is the demo and how to leave it, and no real serial port is opened or listed. ([code](apothecary/demo.py))
- Walkthrough page 00, *Getting started*: install, `apothecary check`, `apothecary openscad install` before the first serve, `apothecary serve --demo`, then the four loops and what each needs; `apothecary --help` ends with the same steps, and `check` with the next one. ([page](walkthrough/00-getting-started.md))
- `apothecary parts import` brings an STL or OBJ in as a part. ([doc](docs/geometry-from-elsewhere.md))
- A `part.json` beside a part's SCAD stands in for a Python wrapper, and such a part's parameters are what its SCAD declares, as OpenSCAD's customizer lists them. ([code](apothecary/projects/parts/customizer.py))
- The garage bench draws its one printer, an Ender 3, and real boards, from their makers' drawings. ([doc](docs/geometry-from-elsewhere.md))
- Personal data stays on the device: the server answers this machine only, and the process reaches nothing but a tool fetch -- OpenSCAD (or its source, on Linux arm64), the slicer, the firmware toolchains and a Rust sketch's crates. ([code](apothecary/stays_local.py))
- `apothecary openscad install` fetches a checksum-verified OpenSCAD snapshot, natively on each platform -- the AppImage on Linux x86_64 (extracted where there is no FUSE), the app from the disk image on macOS, the portable zip on Windows x64 -- and renders use it, with Manifold. ([readme](README.md))
- On Linux arm64 `apothecary openscad install` builds OpenSCAD from source at the snapshot's date, refusing with the `apt install` line when the build lacks something; `--jobs N`. ([code](apothecary/openscad_installer.py))
- `apothecary openscad status` says, for each install, its platform, how it was installed, whether it runs natively, and whether it has Manifold. ([code](apothecary/cli/openscad.py))
- A part can name its STL path and the oldest OpenSCAD it renders with; a build finds the first one new enough. ([code](apothecary/projects/parts/stl_renderer.py))
- Gridfinity bins, from the vendored gridfinity-rebuilt-openscad. ([part](parts/gridfinity/README.md))
- `problems`, `solutions`, `preflight`, `release`, `parts checklist` and `parts verify`; a problem names its pieces by tree path. ([pages](docs/README.md))
- `apothecary kernel-reference` measures the kernel corpus as OpenSCAD's Manifold renders it, the reference a Rust geometry kernel is compared with. ([code](apothecary/cli/kernels.py))
- A Cargo workspace of two stubs under `crates/`, a Rust core and a Rust service, built by neither the suites nor CI. ([readme](crates/apothecary-service/README.md))
- One parameter contract for a part and for a piece made from a picture: `ParamsSpec` and `Validation`, answered by the parts routes and the made routes alike. ([code](apothecary/projects/parts/params.py))
- The `Assembly` model: sites, structures and parts as one recursive tree; `/sites/{name}` carries the whole `tree` beside the flat `structures` list. ([code](apothecary/hierarchy.py))
- The garage: walls, utility stubs, shelving, a CNC router stub, and printer_1 at the bench's left end. ([code](apothecary/example_hierarchy.py))
- The fractal zoom viewer: any site's tree at any depth, its nodes as real geometry -- exact primitives, and parts as their STLs -- coloured by subsystem. ([page](walkthrough/04-serving-it.md))
- The viewer's header is one row: the site, the breadcrumb, ⚙ View, Docs, ⌗ Ring and the site's validity. ([doc](docs/viewer.md))
- Docs, in the header and as the ring's Panels › Docs, opens the documentation in a new tab; `/walkthrough` is an index of the walkthrough's pages, in order. ([doc](docs/viewer.md#the-header))
- The site drop-down, and the ring's Site, put the site in the address, so a reload, a link, Back and Forward land on it; the old site's focus, Machine and variants stay with it. ([doc](docs/viewer.md#the-header))
- ⚙ View says how the world is drawn -- Snap to grid (50 mm), Detail (Full, Black box, Dot), Assembly outlines and Walls selectable -- and the canvas ring's View holds the same with Fit. ([doc](docs/viewer.md#how-the-world-is-drawn))
- Walls are drawn faded, and a click in the world passes through them to what is inside, unless Walls selectable is ticked; a wall's row in Site selects it either way. ([doc](docs/viewer.md#the-world))
- The ring: `m` or a right-click opens the verbs of what it is on, seated on nine keypad cells; the digits pressed are the address, said after every verb and in the tooltip of each button a cell also does. ([code](apothecary/menu.py))
- Every docked panel is in one rail beside the world, on the right until ⇄ or its grip moves it: Site and Selected stacked over a strip of tabs -- Pictures, the Bench, a docked Machine -- one shown at a time; panels close, collapse, float and dock, `~` hides the rail, and its side, width and panels are remembered per browser. ([code](apothecary/static/panels.js))
- The ring's Panels are Site, Selected, Pictures, Machine (a board's, its log in it), Rail, Bench and Docs. ([code](apothecary/menu.py))
- Site: the site's problems in one fold, each selecting its piece at its level (the header's count opens it), the category chips, the Contents tree with a piece in a problem marked, and folds for every site's pins (Pinned, each taken back from its row), the site's jobs and its SCAD. ([test](tests/e2e/test_site_panel.py))
- Badges anchored to their things in the world, as icons -- ▣ a place, 📷 a camera, ⚡ or 🖨 a board -- their words on hover; a click selects the place, the camera, or the machine and opens its Machine. ([code](apothecary/static/anchors.js))
- The part editor: Part › Edit opens a part's parameters in Selected, starting from what is drawn; a staged set is checked once the controls rest, OpenSCAD's errors shown by line, the measured bounds beside the declared ones; Apply, Revert and Defaults. ([page](walkthrough/14-designing-a-part.md))
- A part's renders are kept in a cache of variants by what made them; Apply draws one in its own tab, whose address keeps it, marked apart from the saved part, and the defaults again render nothing; with the part's own numbers staged, Apply draws the saved part even after a SCAD edit, its own SCAD back in the editor. ([page](walkthrough/14-designing-a-part.md))
- The SCAD editor: a part's SCAD under its parameters, checked once typing stops (`/parts/{name}/draft/check`), a failing line marked, an edit rendered after a second of quiet (`/draft/render`) and drawn as an applied variant; Part › Save keeps it as a commit on `refs/apothecary/variants/<part>/<name>`, leaving the checkout alone; Part › Variants, Float and Fullscreen. ([page](walkthrough/16-editing-a-parts-scad.md))
- `apothecary firmware` installs arduino-cli, and compiles and uploads sketches kept with parts, each named with its toolchain (`esp32_blink@arduino`, `esp32_blink@rust-esp32`). ([doc](docs/firmware.md))
- Firmware toolchains are modules behind one interface: Arduino, and Rust for the classic ESP32 (cargo, espflash), which finds ports, listens to a board and asks its chip itself, with no arduino-cli. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#rust-for-the-esp32))
- `parts/esp32_blink`: a sketch that blinks GPIO 2 and announces itself over serial, in Arduino and in Rust (esp-hal, no_std), each saying the same hello. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#rust-for-the-esp32))
- `apothecary firmware install --rust-esp32` fetches rustup, Espressif's Xtensa Rust, rust-src, LLVM and GCC archives (each checked against what its source publishes; no espup) and espflash into the tools dir, and vendors the crates, so every Rust build runs offline. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#rust-for-the-esp32))
- No build path, and so no user name, is in a flashed Rust image: the build remaps every folder and its last step checks the ELF. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#rust-for-the-esp32))
- A board's identity, the sketch last flashed to it, and the sketch it announces over serial; a pin follows its board when the kernel renumbers the port. ([doc](docs/firmware.md))
- `apothecary firmware printer` identifies and polls a G-code printer; its port is opened once and held, and closed with DTR up, so the board is not rebooted. ([doc](docs/firmware.md))
- A printer pinned to its scene node sets that node's status from its polls; pinning one whose link is held polls it once. `GET /sites/{name}/devices` says which links are held. ([doc](docs/firmware.md))
- Selected's Device section is one line for a pinned board -- its port and what it is doing -- with Open, its Machine, and Unpin; for a piece with none, the ports to pin and Query. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#in-the-viewer-one-machine-per-board))
- A printer's Pin, from its Device section or its ring, pins the board inside it -- printer_1's mainboard, where its Device, its Machine and Slice look -- and asks the board once what it is (M115) unless something is known to be printing on that port, the status line saying which; the printer's Device section shows that pin with Unpin beside it. Print and Slice say whether the printer has no board pinned, or one pinned and not connected. A piece made from a picture has no Device section. ([doc](docs/viewer.md#the-ring))
- A board's Machine is the one place for it, printer or devkit, a tab of the rail's strip: its state, its one log with one query box, its controls and where it is pinned; a devkit's says the sketch it should run against the one it is heard saying, what changed since it was flashed, and its serial output once Listen opens its port. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#in-the-viewer-one-machine-per-board))
- A devkit's port is opened only when asked: its Machine opens none, Listen and Probe do (also Device › Link), and Identify asks M115 only when pressed. ([test](tests/e2e/test_one_machine.py))
- A devkit's Machine flashes it: the sketch it should run, its board, Compile, and Compile & upload after asking, the task's output, then Identify's listen for the hello; Device › Flash opens it at that card. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#in-the-viewer-one-machine-per-board))
- A board's verbs from the ring go to its Machine, opened for it when it is not; inside a Machine the ring is its device ring, and its buttons wear that ring's addresses. Device holds Open, Poll, Flash, Query, Unpin, Rescan, Link and, on a printer, Control. ([code](apothecary/menu.py))
- One model of every board the page knows, which polls a board once whatever draws it, writes its state one way on every drawer, and looks for boards again by itself when one it watches goes quiet. ([code](apothecary/static/boards.js))
- A printer's status, temperatures and comms log, close up in its Machine. ([doc](docs/firmware.md))
- Latched printer control: refused unless armed, held to fixed caps; E-STOP always goes. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#control-behind-a-latch))
- Print from here: a kept G-code file, checked, then streamed one line per `ok`. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#printing-without-an-sd-card))
- Bed leveling: the stored mesh read or the bed probed, and each reading kept and drawn. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#bed-leveling))
- A printer's nozzle marker in the world, which follows each poll and moves ahead of a jog. ([doc](docs/firmware.md))
- The Bench: the toolchain modules, the suggested cores, libraries, the sketches compiled or uploaded to a port, raw flash with esptool and the task log, as a tab of the rail; its verbs are the ring's Panels › Bench, whose Install is a ring of the toolchain modules, then the slicer. - A print is a job: one operation a machine performs on a part, kept in the state folder with its kind, machine, site, part, file, times and outcome; a running one marks its printer busy; a kind of job belongs to a kind of machine, and a mill would register its own. ([code](apothecary/jobs.py))

- The Print card names the part a print makes, from the site its printer is pinned in, and its history is the printer's jobs; when a print ends, the status line says so, or that it failed and why. ([doc](docs/firmware.md#a-print-is-a-job))
- `apothecary slicer install` installs a pinned OrcaSlicer from its GitHub releases, checked against the digest GitHub publishes, natively on Linux, macOS and Windows; `apothecary slicer status` says where it stands, and what is planned. ([doc](docs/slicer.md))

### Changed

- Every scene object says what it is (`type`): `/render` and the CLI refuse one with no type or an unknown one, where 0.1.0 rebuilt it from its shape. ([doc](docs/scene-json.md))
- Every part is a folder named with underscores, `parts/<name>/<name>.scad`, in category folders where it has kin (`parts/boards/`). ([doc](docs/parts-authoring.md))
- `apothecary parts generate-stl` keeps an STL that is newer than everything it was built from, at the same parameters, unless `--force`, and takes `--param`; `serve` builds the missing STLs in the background. ([code](apothecary/cli/parts.py))
- `/` and `/viewer` open the fractal viewer on the garage site. ([page](walkthrough/04-serving-it.md))
- `apothecary test` is `run` -- the ordinary command, the unit tests and the walkthrough -- and `all`, which adds every browser test, each exiting as pytest does. ([doc](CONTRIBUTING.md))
- `serve` and `photo view` refuse a port another server holds before they print an address -- "port in use (is apothecary serve running?) -- pass --port" -- and `serve --help` shows its defaults. ([code](apothecary/cli/server.py))
- `apothecary check` reports each part's OpenSCAD, the slicer and each firmware toolchain, exits 1 when a required package is missing, and ends with the next step. ([readme](README.md))
- `apothecary --help` lists a person's commands, then the repository's own, and ends with where to start. ([code](apothecary/cli/main.py))
- Playwright is a dev dependency, and ruff is the one lint configuration. ([doc](CONTRIBUTING.md))
- README.md is the entry doc, starting at walkthrough page 00; QUICKSTART.md points at it; CONTRIBUTING.md names the CI gates. ([readme](README.md))
